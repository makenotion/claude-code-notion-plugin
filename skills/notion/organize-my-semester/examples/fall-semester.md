# Organize three courses into a study desk

This fictional example shows a learner's notes and deadlines becoming connected
course pages and a useful daily starting point. Adapt its structure and visual
choices to the learner's subjects and preferences.

## Starting material

> Organize my fall semester. I want to know what to work on next, then get straight
> to the relevant notes. Keep it quiet and warm, with no cover image. Don't solve
> the assignments for me.
>
> Courses: Biology, room B204, office hours Tuesday 14:00–15:00; Design studio,
> room Studio 4, office hours Thursday 11:00–12:00; Statistics, room C110,
> office hours not announced. My immediate priority is the membrane essay outline.
> Remind me to book time to photograph my studio model and ask about statistics

## Connected pages

The sidebar contains one **📚 Fall semester** home with three child destinations:
**Courses**, **Assignments**, and **Notes**. The home embeds views of these
collections; it does not create a second copy of the assignments.

- **Courses:** Biology, Design studio, and Statistics. Each has room, office
  hours when known, related notes, and related assignments.
- **Assignments:** the five items below, each related to its course. Dates and
  weights stay empty when the source does not provide them.
- **Notes:** the two supplied lecture/studio notes. Each keeps its course,
  original date, source filename, and substantive note body.

## The study desk on arrival

Use a full-width page with the default reading font, a 📚 icon, and no cover.
Give Biology a green 🌿 identity, Design studio a brown 🎨 identity, and
Statistics a blue 📊 identity. Carry those icons into course mentions and notes.
Use muted brown section headings and one pale yellow priority callout.

Below the title, a compact navigation row offers **Courses · Assignments · Notes**.
A narrow left column occupies roughly one quarter of the content width. The
remaining space holds three small course cards and the Upcoming assignment table.
The cards have no image previews, wrapped titles, and brief useful summaries.

| Left column: This week                                                                                                                                    | Main column: Your courses, then Assignments                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Start here** — Membrane essay outline. Compare the two transport mechanisms in your October 28 notes before drafting your outline. Open the assignment. | **🌿 Biology** — B204 · Tue 14:00–15:00. Next due: Membrane essay outline · Nov 3.                            |
| **Reminders** — Book model photography time. Ask about statistics office hours.                                                                           | **🎨 Design studio** — Studio 4 · Thu 11:00–12:00. Next due: Light model critique · Nov 5.                    |
| **Add an assignment** — a configured creation action for Assignments.                                                                                     | **📊 Statistics** — C110. Next due: Confidence interval exercise · Nov 6.                                     |
| **Plan your week** — a collapsed space for the learner's own plan.                                                                                        | **Upcoming** — Membrane essay outline, Light model critique, and Confidence interval exercise, in that order. |

Course cards open their course pages. Derive each next-due summary from its
related, incomplete, dated assignments so the summary changes with the work.
The Upcoming table shows **Assignment, Course, Due, Status** with full dates and
readable titles; weight remains in the assignment's details. Keep the first
assignment rows visible with the course cards, without a long welcome message.

Use alternate views of the same Assignments collection:

- **Calendar:** the three assignments with known due dates.
- **No due date:** unfinished assignments with an empty Due property; Sampling methods reading.
- **Submitted:** Material journal.

Upcoming excludes Submitted and undated items. The visible No due date tab keeps
reading that has no deadline easy to find.

## Inside Biology

The course page opens with the green 🌿 icon, **Biology** title, room B204, and
Tuesday office hours. Its body starts with useful learning context:

> **Membranes and transport**
>
> Selective permeability affects which substances cross a membrane. Facilitated
> diffusion uses transport proteins to move substances down a concentration
> gradient. Active transport requires energy to move substances against a
> gradient. A transport protein alone does not tell us which process is happening.
>
> Source: Biology notes, October 28.

A short **Suggested study questions** section follows:

- What information would you need to distinguish facilitated diffusion from
  active transport in a diagram?
- How would you explain the role of a concentration gradient in your own words?

These are suggested questions, not answers to the graded essay. A **Course notes**
view opens the original October 28 note, preserving its wording and source. A
**Biology assignments** view contains only Membrane essay outline. **Back to Fall
semester** returns to the home.

## Inside Membrane essay outline

Use a 📝 icon. Show **Due: November 3, 2026**, **Status: In progress**, and the
**🌿 Biology** course relation near the title. Keep **Weight: 10%** and
**Source: Biology syllabus** in details.

The body contains:

### What to submit

An outline comparing passive and active transport. Include a thesis, two
supporting ideas, and citations to the course readings. This is an outline,
not a completed essay.

### Prepare your outline

- Revisit **Biology notes · October 28** and identify the distinctions you want
  your outline to address.
- Find the assigned reading list before choosing citations. It has not yet been
  added to these materials.
- Draft your own thesis and two supporting points, then check that each point
  connects to a course reading.

### Before submitting

- [ ] Thesis compares passive and active transport.
- [ ] Two supporting ideas are included.
- [ ] Citations refer to assigned course readings.

**Still needed:** assigned reading list.

The course relation leads back to Biology, and a **Back to Fall semester** mention
returns to the study desk. The outline remains In progress; creating this page
does not mark any preparation step complete.

## Other assignment details

**Light model critique** retains the brief and the October 29 studio note. Its
next step is to book photography time. Its preparation checklist covers building
the model, taking three comparable photographs, and annotating the decisions.

**Confidence interval exercise** retains the calculation and interpretation
requirements. Its next step is to obtain the dataset; no values or answers are
invented. **Sampling methods reading** keeps its due date and weight empty.
**Material journal** remains Submitted and is reachable through that view. Its
page records “Submitted October 27”; its Due property stays empty because the
brief does not supply a due date.

The learner can choose a course, read actual material, open the next assignment,
and return to upcoming work without first filling in empty pages.

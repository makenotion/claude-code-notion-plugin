# Design a personal study space

The learner's habits and subject material determine the page. A semester of
courses, a single research seminar, and exam revision need different homes.

Use a personal scale, a subject-relevant cover when useful, and a recognizable
icon. Keep reading comfortable with clear headings and dividers; add widgets
only when they serve the learner’s work.

## Write labels for the learner

Use familiar labels such as “Courses,” “Notes,” “Assignments,” and “No due date.”
For a weekly planning section, prefer “Plan your week” to “Weekly reset.” Keep
existing course names and clear labels the learner already uses. Write buttons
as actions, such as “Add an assignment” or “Add a note,” when those actions exist.
For a static course summary, say “Updated [date]; check Assignments for changes”
using the actual update date. Do not leave “snapshot” unexplained or imply that
static text updates automatically. Keep setup terms such as “record” and
“relation” out of study instructions.

## Make the desk fit the learner

For several courses, build an asymmetric study desk: a compact rail for reminders
and capture actions, a course gallery in the main area, and the next assignments
close beneath it. Make the cards useful with subject identity, the next deadline,
and a route into course material. For a single course, use a continuous reading
page with notes and work close together. Choose the composition for the learner's
work. Preserve citations for the learning material itself.

Give subjects stable visual identities, such as a botanical green for biology
and a warm paper tone for design history, when consistent with the user's taste.
Use subject titles and an occasional illustration or cover that belongs to the
material. Choose a reading font that suits the material. Avoid motivational slogans and
an obligatory pair of “focus” and “keep in mind” boxes.

Put an actionable study prompt beside its source explanation. A biology learner
should encounter the actual distinction they are learning, not just a link to
“notes.” Keep assignments in a compact working view near the notes; show due and
undated work explicitly. Do not elevate a generic task board over the learning.

Personal capture areas can be deliberately unfinished: a question prompt invites
writing. Course pages and imported lecture notes must already contain substantive
material. Suggested questions are labeled, grades and progress are not invented.

## Judge the result

Open a course, an assignment, and the return route. Can the learner see what to
study and begin without setup? Check note readability, subject identity, and
upcoming/undated work. On a narrow screen, course navigation should precede the
study area in a sensible order. Inspect the chosen typography, subject color, and imagery together; disclose any
unperformed visual inspection.

Set page width and typography deliberately. Give the study desk its own home;
keep an optional index separate from the study desk.
